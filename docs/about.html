About Cho

An open-source CTF workspace that keeps the tools, the terminal, and the AI in one place — and on your machine.

⚠ Cho is for authorised CTF competitions and isolated practice labs. It assists; it does not automate attacks against systems you don't have permission to test, and it never promises a flag.

What it is

Cho is a terminal-style desktop app that helps you work a Capture-the-Flag challenge from start to finish.

You drop in the challenge files or a link, and Cho gives you three things in one window: a real shell in the challenge folder, a catalogue of 100+ security tools with ready-to-run commands, and Cho — an AI teammate that runs locally and talks you through the next step. The same prompt runs a command or answers a question, so you stay in the terminal the whole time.

Why it exists

CTF players juggle a dozen scattered tools, a pile of notes, and a browser full of cheat-sheets. Beginners don't know which tool to reach for; everyone loses track of what they've already tried. AI assistants can help, but most send your files to the cloud and happily state guesses as facts.

Cho is the answer to that: one workspace, a local model so nothing leaves your machine, and an assistant that is honest about what it knows — it marks a finding VERIFIED only when real command output backs it, and ASSUMPTION otherwise.

Who it's for

  • Newcomers — learn by watching Cho pick the right tool and explain the output, step by step.
  • Competitors — a fast cockpit for triage, recon, and keeping every challenge's work organised.
  • Teachers & clubs — a safe, self-contained lab that needs no cloud account and runs offline.

Principles

Local-first

The AI and the tools run on your own hardware. Your challenges and files never leave the machine.

Transparent

Verified facts are separated from guesses. No invented output, no hidden steps.

Controlled

Work stays in the challenge folder and the hosts you authorise. Destructive commands are blocked; network commands wait for your approval.

Honest about limits

No claim to support every tool or to win competitions. The docs state exactly what each safeguard does and doesn't cover.

How it's built

An Electron desktop shell with a sandboxed interface and a Node core that runs the file analysis, classifies every command, enforces the authorised-target scope, and relays to a local Ollama model. The security tools run through Kali (WSL) or a throwaway Docker container. Full design notes are in the architecture doc.
